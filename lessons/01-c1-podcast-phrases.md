title: C1 podcast phrases
level: C1
description: Host-style language for intros, promises, and serious conversation.

## Words

### water down
**Definition:** To make something weaker, simpler, or less intense so it is easier or less controversial.
**Synonyms:** dilute, weaken, tone down, soften
**Example:** Don’t water down the argument just to keep everyone happy.
**IPA:** /ˈwɔːtə daʊn/
**Notes:** Often used about opinions, news, alcohol, or teaching material.

### stick to
**Definition:** To continue using or following one thing instead of switching.
**Synonyms:** keep to, stay with, hold to, adhere to
**Example:** I’ll stick to the original plan, even if it takes longer.
**IPA:** /stɪk tuː/
**Notes:** Close alternatives: “keep to,” “stay with.”

### frankly
**Definition:** Used before an honest, sometimes blunt, opinion.
**Synonyms:** to be honest, to be frank, honestly, in all honesty
**Example:** Frankly, I don’t think this idea will work.
**IPA:** /ˈfræŋkli/
**Notes:** “Fankly” is a misspelling. “To be frank” is a close alternative.

### keen
**Definition:** Very interested in something, or eager to do it.
**Synonyms:** eager, enthusiastic, interested, up for
**Example:** She’s keen on photography and spends every weekend shooting.
**IPA:** /kiːn/
**Notes:** Common in British English: “keen on + noun/gerund.”

### mates
**Definition:** Informal British English for friends.
**Synonyms:** friends, pals, buddies
**Example:** I’m meeting my mates after work.
**IPA:** /meɪts/

### gauntlet
**Definition:** A challenge, especially one that dares someone to prove themselves. From the idiom “throw down the gauntlet.”
**Synonyms:** challenge, dare
**Example:** By setting a tight deadline, they threw down the gauntlet.
**IPA:** /ˈɡɔːntlət/

### lexicon
**Definition:** The set of words and phrases used in a particular subject, group, or situation.
**Synonyms:** vocabulary, terminology, wording, jargon
**Example:** Legal documents have a lexicon of their own.
**IPA:** /ˈleksɪkən/

### nuances
**Definition:** Small, often subtle differences in meaning, feeling, or tone.
**Synonyms:** subtleties, fine distinctions, shades of meaning
**Example:** Once you know the nuances, “sorry” and “excuse me” are not interchangeable.
**IPA:** /ˈnjuːɑːnsɪz/

### pleasantries
**Definition:** Polite, light remarks used to be friendly, not to discuss anything serious.
**Synonyms:** small talk, polite remarks, niceties
**Example:** After a few pleasantries, they got down to business.
**IPA:** /ˈplezəntriz/

## Phrases

### whether you're tuning in for the first time
**Definition:** A host phrase for “if this is the first time you are watching or listening.”
**Synonyms:** if you're new here, whether this is your first listen, if you've just joined
**Example:** Whether you’re tuning in for the first time or you’ve been here for years, welcome.
**Notes:** “Tune in” comes from radio; it now also means joining a podcast, stream, or episode.

### we're genuinely thrilled you're here
**Definition:** A warm, sincere way to say you are very glad someone has joined.
**Synonyms:** we're so glad you're here, it's great to have you, we're really pleased you've joined
**Example:** We’re genuinely thrilled you’re here — thanks for spending the hour with us.
**Notes:** “Genuinely” stresses that the feeling is real, not just polite.

### you're following along
**Definition:** You are keeping up with the episode, course, or explanation as it goes.
**Synonyms:** you're keeping up, you're coming with us, you're tracking this
**Example:** If you’re following along at home, pause and try the example yourself.
**Notes:** Also used as an invitation: “Follow along with me.”

### would mean the world
**Definition:** Would be extremely valuable or appreciated.
**Synonyms:** would mean a lot, would be hugely appreciated, we'd be so grateful
**Example:** A share with a friend who is learning English would mean the world.
**Notes:** Often used when asking for a small favour: subscribe, review, tell a friend.

### who's keen on leveling up
**Definition:** Someone who is eager to improve, especially to a higher level.
**Synonyms:** who wants to improve, who's eager to get better, who's looking to step up
**Example:** This episode is for anyone who’s keen on leveling up their listening.
**Notes:** British: “keen on.” American spelling: “leveling”; British: “levelling.”

### couldn't have put it better
**Definition:** That is exactly the right way to say it; I fully agree with the wording.
**Synonyms:** that's exactly it, well said, that captures it perfectly, I agree completely
**Example:** “It’s exhausting, not difficult.” I couldn’t have put it better.
**Notes:** The usual form is “couldn’t have put it better,” not “couldn’t have it put it better.”

### we're throwing down the gauntlet for C1 listeners
**Definition:** We are issuing a challenge aimed at advanced (C1) listeners.
**Synonyms:** we're issuing a challenge, we're laying down a challenge, we're daring C1 listeners
**Example:** This week we’re throwing down the gauntlet for C1 listeners: no slow speech, no subtitles.
**Notes:** Full idiom: “throw down the gauntlet.” A gauntlet was a metal glove; throwing it down meant challenging someone to a fight.

### going to immerse yourselves
**Definition:** You are going to surround yourselves with the language so you take it in deeply.
**Synonyms:** going to dive into, going to surround yourselves with, going to get stuck into
**Example:** For the next twenty minutes, you’re going to immerse yourselves in natural conversation.
**Notes:** “Immerse yourself in” is the usual pattern.

### the kind of exchanges you'll overhear
**Definition:** The sort of real conversations you might catch around you, not scripted classroom talk.
**Synonyms:** the sort of conversations you'd catch, the chatter you'd hear around you, talk you'd pick up in passing
**Example:** These are the kind of exchanges you’ll overhear in a café, not in a textbook.
**Notes:** An “exchange” here is a short conversation. To “overhear” is to hear something not aimed at you.

### while catching up with mates down at the pub
**Definition:** While chatting with friends at the pub and sharing news.
**Synonyms:** while chatting with friends at the pub, while having a pint with friends, while catching up over a drink
**Example:** You’d hear this while catching up with mates down at the pub, not in a job interview.
**Notes:** “Catch up with” = talk after some time apart. “Down at the pub” is informal British English.

### we will be weaving
**Definition:** We will be mixing something through the episode, rather than treating it as a separate block.
**Synonyms:** we'll be mixing in, we'll thread through, we'll work in
**Example:** We will be weaving these expressions through a longer discussion, so you hear them in context.
**Notes:** Often “weave in” or “weave through”: “We’ll weave in a few idioms as we go.”

### pick up on the nuances
**Definition:** To notice the small differences in meaning, tone, or implication.
**Synonyms:** notice the subtleties, catch the fine distinctions, sense the shades of meaning
**Example:** Native speakers pick up on the nuances that learners often miss.
**Notes:** “Pick up on” = notice and respond to something that is not fully spelled out.

### you'll have packed your
**Definition:** By the end, you will have filled your (notes, bag, vocabulary) with a lot of material. The sentence is usually finished with an object: bags, notebook, toolkit.
**Synonyms:** you'll have filled your, you'll have loaded your, you'll have stocked your
**Example:** By the end of the episode, you’ll have packed your notes with phrases you can actually use.
**Notes:** Complete it: “you’ll have packed your bags / notebook / toolkit with…”

### loads of practical
**Definition:** A large amount of useful, real-world language or advice. Usually finished with a noun: phrases, tips, examples.
**Synonyms:** lots of useful, plenty of hands-on, a great deal of practical
**Example:** You’ll get loads of practical phrases for meetings and small talk.
**Notes:** “Loads of” is informal British English for “a lot of.” Complete it: “loads of practical English / tips / examples.”

### put to use straight away
**Definition:** Use immediately, not later or only in theory.
**Synonyms:** use immediately, use right away, put into practice at once
**Example:** These are chunks you can put to use straight away in your next conversation.
**Notes:** Also: “put to use right away,” “use straight away.”

### let's dive straight in, shall we?
**Definition:** A friendly way to start immediately, without a long introduction. The tag “shall we?” invites the listener along.
**Synonyms:** let's jump right in, let's get stuck in, shall we begin
**Example:** Enough preamble — let’s dive straight in, shall we?
**Notes:** Soft, British-sounding invitation. “Dive in” = start in depth.

### let's kick things off
**Definition:** Let’s start; let’s begin the first part.
**Synonyms:** let's get started, let's begin, let's open with
**Example:** Let’s kick things off with a question: when did you last change your mind in a debate?
**Notes:** Informal. Close alternatives: “let’s get started,” “let’s begin.”

### when you're not just exchanging pleasantries
**Definition:** When the conversation has moved past hello and small talk into something more substantial.
**Synonyms:** when you're past small talk, when you're having a real conversation, when you're not just being polite
**Example:** This language matters when you’re not just exchanging pleasantries, but actually disagreeing.
**Notes:** Use it to contrast light chat with a real discussion.

### dissecting ideas
**Definition:** Examining ideas in detail, taking them apart to understand them.
**Synonyms:** analysing ideas, breaking ideas down, examining ideas closely
**Example:** In this seminar we won’t summarise the article; we’ll be dissecting ideas.
**Notes:** Slightly academic or analytical. “Break down” is a simpler alternative.

### articulating your stance on issues
**Definition:** Expressing your position on topics clearly and carefully.
**Synonyms:** expressing your position, spelling out where you stand, making your view clear
**Example:** C1 speakers should be comfortable articulating their stance on issues like housing or climate policy.
**Notes:** “Stance” = your position. “Articulate” = express clearly, not just have an opinion.

### engaging in debates
**Definition:** Taking part in discussions where people argue for different sides.
**Synonyms:** taking part in arguments, joining discussions, debating
**Example:** She is used to engaging in debates at work, not just agreeing to keep the peace.
**Notes:** “Engage in” = participate in, often in a serious way.

### particular lexicon that tends to crop up
**Definition:** A specific set of words that often appears in that kind of talk.
**Synonyms:** specific vocabulary that often comes up, wording that tends to appear, language that keeps coming up
**Example:** Political podcasts have a particular lexicon that tends to crop up: “mandate,” “turnout,” “on the fence.”
**Notes:** “Crop up” = appear, often unexpectedly or repeatedly.
